# AML transaction typologies (reference — not scoring rules)

Use these as *definitions to reason with*, not as automatic triggers. A pattern is only a concern if the
evidence genuinely fits and the client has no legitimate explanation.

- **Structuring ("smurfing")** — many cash deposits *just under* a reporting threshold (~£10,000),
  clustered within days and together exceeding it. Splitting one large deposit to dodge reporting.
- **Layering** — rapid onward transfers to several *distinct* counterparties shortly after funds arrive.
  Moving money through hops to obscure its origin.
- **Round-tripping** — money sent out, then a ~matching amount returns via a *different* counterparty
  within a short window. Circular flow to fabricate legitimacy.
- **Dormant-then-spike** — a long inactive period, then a sudden burst of large transactions. An account
  waking up abnormally.

Legitimate explanations exist (house sale proceeds, internal treasury settlements, documented trade), so
weigh the client's evidence before concluding.
