1 · What it is & the problem it solves

Compliance and risk analysts manually review huge volumes of fragmented signals — KYC records, transaction streams, sanctions/PEP lists, adverse media — under roughly 95% false-positive noise, and scoring is inconsistent from one analyst to the next. This POC ingests a multi-source dossier per customer and produces one prioritised, risk-scored, explained, auditable triage queue, so analysts spend attention where it matters.

The standout idea: danger rarely lives in one signal — it lives in the correlation across them (a PEP + a high-risk-country wire + fresh adverse media). The engine aggregates people, not rows, and never chokes — even on deliberately broken dossiers.

Rubric optimised: 40% data-integration + AI-reasoning accuracy · 30% risk-summary clarity + prioritisation · 15% presentation · 15% creativity/reflection.

Which LLM?

NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B (nvidia/nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b) — a large reasoning model served on NVIDIA's OpenAI-compatible endpoint, called via instructor in JSON mode against our Pydantic schema. Temperature 0. Chosen for its high rate limits and strong reasoning.

The model is an advisory, independent second opinion — it is deliberately not told the rules score, so agreement is meaningful. It never writes the score arithmetic.

  • Multi-step (see §6b): a 5-node LangGraph graph, not one call — three parallel domain analysts → synthesis → adversarial verification.
  • Provider-swappable via config.py — nvidia ↔ gemini (Gemini 2.5/3 Flash) ↔ anthropic (Claude Haiku); the schema and never-fails wrapper are shared.
  • Disk-cached findings (keyed by provider+model+prompt hash) → instant, rate-limit-proof re-runs.
  • Offline fallback: no key → a deterministic simulated second opinion so the demo still shows confidence + auto-clear.

4 · The data — 5 families per customer

20 seeded synthetic customers (seed 42, fixed reference date → byte-identical output; no real PII). Class balance is deliberately inverted vs the real ~0.1% suspicious rate so every band is exercised (6 LOW · 7 MED · 5 HIGH · 2 critical).

FamilyContentsExample signals
KYC / identityname, DOB, nationality, occupation, id doc, onboarded, completenessmissing ID, high-risk occupation
Customer risk factorsgeography, product, PEP status, entity type, tenurehigh-risk jurisdiction, PEP, new account
Transactionsbenign base stream + at most one injected typologystructuring, layering, round-trip, dormant-spike
Screeningsanctions (exact vs fuzzy match_score), PEP, adverse mediaOFAC match, negative news
Robustness metadatamissing / extra document flagsthe "never-fails" testers

Four typologies are injected as pure functions and detected temporally (proximity in time, not amount alone). The deterministic engine flags them on every customer, and the LLM transactions-analyst is independently prompted to hunt for the same four in the raw transactions and correspondence — so each pattern is caught both by arithmetic and semantically:

TypologyWhat it meansDetector rule
Structuring ("smurfing")Many cash deposits just under the reporting floor, clustered in a short window and together exceeding it — splitting one big deposit to dodge reporting.≥3 cash deposits in [0.8×floor, floor) within 7 days summing > £10,000 floor
LayeringRapid onward transfers to several distinct counterparties in a short window — moving money through hops to obscure its origin.≥3 outbound transfers to distinct counterparties within the window
Round-tripMoney goes out, then a ~matching amount returns via a different counterparty within a window — circular flow to fabricate legitimacy.out then in of ~equal amount (within tolerance), different counterparty, inside the window
Dormant-spikeA long inactive gap, then a sudden burst of large transactions — an account waking up abnormally.gap ≥ dormant-days, then ≥ min-burst transactions above the burst-amount floor

In engine_mode=llm these are shown as corroborating evidence in the case view (with the exact txn IDs, amounts, and window) and aggregated in the dashboard "Detected patterns" chart; only a sanctions match is a hard rail. In engine_mode=hybrid each contributes weighted points to the rules score directly.

5 · Scoring — deterministic, order matters

Each factor is a pure function returning a Finding | None. The order of operations is non-negotiable: a hard signal must never be averaged away.

flowchart TD
  F["Findings from FACTOR_RULES + TYPOLOGY_RULES"] --> O{"Any override?
sanctions exact match · PEP in high-risk geo"}
  O -- yes --> H["score = 100 · band = HIGH"]
  O -- no --> W["weighted additive sum → normalise 0–100"]
  W --> B{"band cutoffs"}
  B -->|"≤ 35"| LOW["🟢 LOW"]
  B -->|"36–65"| MED["🟡 MED"]
  B -->|"66–100"| HIGH["🔴 HIGH"]
  H --> DR["drivers = contributions that SUM to the score"]
  LOW --> DR
  MED --> DR
  HIGH --> DR

Every Finding carries evidence (transaction ids, amounts, window) — that is simultaneously the analyst rationale and the audit trail. Money is Decimal; no wall-clock or randomness in any predicate.

6b · Multi-step AI orchestration (LangGraph)

A single LLM call is fragile — one bad parse or hallucination and the whole assessment is wrong. Instead the second opinion is a 5-step graph where no single call decides the outcome and each node degrades independently.

flowchart LR
  S(("START")) --> K["analyze_kyc"]
  S --> T["analyze_transactions"]
  S --> SC["analyze_screening"]
  K --> SY["synthesize
correlate domains"]
  T --> SY
  SC --> SY
  SY --> V["verify
adversarial QA"]
  V --> F["finalize
deterministic package"]
  F --> E(("END"))
  • Fan-out (parallel): three domain analysts — KYC, transactions, screening — each rate only their own domain.
  • Synthesize: correlates the three, weighing how signals reinforce across domains (the real risk).
  • Verify (chain-of-verification): a QA node adversarially re-checks the score against the evidence and corrects it.
  • Finalize: deterministic packaging, robust to any missing upstream step.

LangGraph StateGraph with parallel fan-out via Annotated[list, operator.add] reducers, LangChain structured output, cascade graph → single-call → rules-only. Still confidence-gated against the deterministic rules — the graph only sharpens the advisory opinion, it never overrides the auditable number.

7 · Human-in-the-loop routing

Confidence-gated triage with a kill-switch that fires before any score arithmetic.

flowchart TD
  S["score · flags · confidence · path"] --> K{"kill-switch flag?
SANCTIONS_MATCH · PEP_HIGH_GEO"}
  K -- yes --> ESC["🔴 ESCALATE → MLRO
sign-off required"]
  K -- no --> SC{"score"}
  SC -->|"< 15"| AC["🟢 AUTO_CLEAR"]
  SC -->|"15–39"| JR["🟡 REVIEW · junior"]
  SC -->|"40–69"| SR["🟡 REVIEW · senior"]
  SC -->|"≥ 70"| ESC2["🔴 ESCALATE · senior
sign-off required"]
  AC --> D{"degraded path OR
missing data OR low conf?"}
  D -- yes --> JR
  D -- no --> DONE["🟢 auto-cleared, logged"]
AUTO_CLEAR low risk + high confidence → handled with zero analyst time (human-on-the-loop).
ESCALATE high risk or kill-switch → mandatory human review + maker-checker sign-off (human-in-the-loop).

Confidence = rules↔model agreement. Disagreement, a degraded path, or a dossier with missing data caps confidence and forbids auto-clear — it degrades, it never blanks.

8 · Explainability & audit

Additive drivers (our SHAP)

Each rule contributes signed points that sum exactly to the score (SHAP's local-accuracy property, hand-rolled, zero dependencies, fully deterministic). Rendered as per-driver bars in the case view — this is the analyst explanation and the audit evidence, unified. No shap/LIME (LIME is non-deterministic → disqualifying for compliance).

Append-only audit trail

One AuditRecord per decision — clears and escalates alike, because regulators inspect whether closures are defensible. Each record carries who / what / why / when, the driver breakdown, the ruleset version, and a sha256 input fingerprint for reproducibility. Sign-off is captured on every escalation.

8b · Scaling to 10,000+ / day

The key move: separate the cheap deterministic layer from the expensive LLM layer, and only spend the LLM where it changes the decision. Full detail in docs/SCALING.md.

LayerCost / customer10k/day
Deterministic rules~0.6 ms~6 seconds total (measured: ~1,700/sec ≈ 147M/day/core)
Multi-step LLM graph~60 s (5 calls)infeasible on everyone → gated to the ~10–20% ambiguous middle

Four levers, each already implemented as a working local stand-in:

  • Gating (pipeline.should_crosscheck) — rules auto-clear LOW and escalate HIGH; the LLM runs only on the MED band.
  • Parallelism (pipeline.py, ThreadPoolExecutor) — overlap I/O-bound LLM calls; concurrency-capped for rate limits.
  • Caching (data/llm_cache.json → Redis) — never re-score an unchanged customer.
  • Precomputed read store (store.py SQLite → Postgres) — the dashboard queries results, never re-scores on load.

At 100k/day: swap SQLite→Postgres, threads→Celery/Kafka consumers, JSON cache→Redis. At 1M+/day: shard by region, add a cheaper/distilled model for the gated tier, batch LLM calls.

  • Research first: a 6-agent parallel Workflow studied production AML scoring, deterministic rule engines, LLM guardrails, HITL routing, Streamlit patterns, and synthetic-data generation, then synthesised a single decision brief (docs/research/RESEARCH_BRIEF.md).
  • Spine sequential, leaves parallel: the shared schema (spine) was built single-owner; the independent leaves (docs/deck) were delegated to a background subagent.
